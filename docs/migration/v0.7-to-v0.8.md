# Migration guide: v0.7 → v0.8

v0.8.0 is a minor release of `dnd5e-engine`, `dnd5e-srd-data` and
`nat20-bridge`, in lockstep. Every new name and field is additive and
defaulted, and a payload a host stored under 0.7.0 (specs, intents, an
outcome, an event log) still validates on the 0.8.0 models. What a host can
notice is a handful of behaviour changes: a `move` takes a detour, the monster
AI's closing walk no longer shares cells, an item that casts a spell targets as
that spell, kills are credited to the creature that dealt them, and the new
`KnockedOut` event. The dataset does not change.

## Upgrade checklist

Upgrade the three packages together: `dnd5e-engine` requires
`dnd5e-srd-data>=0.8.0`, and `nat20-bridge` requires both at `>=0.8.0`.
Then:

1. **Handle `KnockedOut`** if you match `CombatEvent` exhaustively: it is a new
   member of the closed union. It is emitted only when a host asks for a
   knock-out.
2. **Handle `knock_out_not_melee`** if you match
   `IntentRejectedError.RejectionReason` exhaustively.
3. **Stop mirroring the old `blocked_path` refusal.** A `move` to an adjacent
   cell behind a wall now walks around it when it can; a host that rejects such
   a move up front (a move preview, a destination filter) needs the same
   change.
4. **List an item that casts a spell with the spell's own targeting.** A Wand of
   Fireballs now places Fireball's Sphere and a Wand of Magic Missiles splits
   its darts; a host that catalogued such items as single-target should stop.
5. **Read `DeathRecord.killer_id` as the creature that dealt the blow**, not the
   creature whose turn it was.
6. Optionally adopt the new features: party-side allies
   (`EncounterMemberSpec.side`), a host-chosen monster target
   (`advance_monster_turn(target_id=...)`) and knock-outs
   (`PlayerIntent.knock_out`).

## Grid movement

### What a host has to do

Nothing, unless it mirrors the engine's movement rules: see the checklist.

### Behaviour changes

- **A `move` takes a detour.** When the direct step to an adjacent destination
  crosses a wall or cuts a blocked corner, the move takes the shortest legal
  route around, priced and walked like any other route: it pays the longer
  route's movement and can provoke opportunity attacks on the way. The
  refusals are now:
    - `blocked_path`: the destination is adjacent, the direct step is illegal,
      and no route goes around;
    - `insufficient_movement`: the detour costs more than the movement left;
      nothing moves;
    - `unreachable`: no legal route, as before.
- **The monster AI's closing walk honours occupancy.** It passes through
  allies, never ends on another creature and never enters an enemy's cell. It
  stops on the first cell of its route that is in range with line of sight
  when that cell is free, else on the cheapest free cell in range; with no
  creature in the way the walk is the same cell for cell as before. It walks
  before it Dashes: it takes the Dash only when no free cell in range is
  within its remaining movement, and the Dash is priced to the cell it stops
  on, not to the target's own cell. When no free cell in range is reachable
  even with a Dash, it walks as far as its movement goes and stops on the last
  free cell; with no route to the target at all it stays where it is. An
  opportunity attack that stops it part-way can still leave it on an ally's
  cell it was passing through (BACKLOG.md).
- **Walled maps are faster.** Wall segments are indexed by the cells they
  touch, so a step or sight test reads only nearby walls. Every answer is
  unchanged; the cost per step no longer grows with the number of walls.

## Item casts

### What a host has to do

List an item whose use casts a spell with that spell's targeting (checklist
item 4).

### Behaviour changes

- A `use_item` whose one resolved activity is a `cast` targets the way the
  spell it casts does. The spell's area is placed, with `AreaTargeted`, and
  catches whoever the spell would, allies and the caster included. A count
  that scales (Magic Missile's darts, extra targets) is read at the item's
  cast level: the level its extra charges buy, else the item's printed level,
  else the spell's own. More `target_ids` than the count allows is refused
  with `CastFailed(reason="target_invalid")` before any charge is spent.
- A spell with an attack roll still places no area, a feature's cast is
  unchanged, and an item with several activities and no `activity_id` still
  places nothing.
- Item text that changes the spell's area or targets (the Wand of Fear's
  60-foot Cone) is not honoured yet, and an item's cast is not range-gated
  (BACKLOG.md).

## Sides

### What a host has to do

Nothing, unless it seats allies. With no `side` on any spec a combat runs
exactly as on 0.7.

### New behaviour

- `EncounterMemberSpec.side` is `"encounter"` (the default) or `"party"`. A
  party-side member joins `party_ids`: foes target it, it fights foes, and
  every ally rule reads it as the party's. On its own turn
  `advance_monster_turn` runs the AI for it, or the host commands it through
  `submit_player_intent` as it would a summon.
- It is not a character: at 0 Hit Points it dies as a monster does, and its
  `DeathRecord.target_kind` is `"npc"` or `"monster"`. A host that assumed a
  party-side death is always a character's must accept those kinds.
- XP is divided among the surviving characters only; an ally takes no share.
  `residual_hp` reports an ally's Hit Points with the party's.
- `start_combat` raises `ValueError` unless at least one encounter member
  fights on the encounter side.
- A standing ally keeps the party in the fight: a combat closed after every
  character has died while an ally still stands reads `ended_reason`
  `"forced"` (or `"flee"` when every foe still in the fight has fled), not
  `"defeat_tpk"`.

## A host-chosen monster target

### What a host has to do

Nothing: `target_id=None` keeps the engine's lowest-HP pick.

### New behaviour

- `advance_monster_turn(handle, target_id=...)` names the creature the acting
  monster attacks, or targets with a stat-block cast. The monster walks toward
  it, Dashing when it must, and never substitutes another creature.
- The id must be in the monster's own target pool: an enemy in the initiative
  order, alive, not knocked out, not its charmer. Any other id, and any id on a
  summon's turn, raises `IntentRejectedError("target_invalid")` before the
  turn starts: no die is rolled, nothing is spent, no event is emitted, and the
  turn stays where it was. On a character's turn `not_actor_turn` is raised
  first.
- An area action keeps the engine's own aim. With `legendary=True` the keyword
  names the legendary action's target, checked before `LegendaryActionUsed`.

## Knocking out a creature

### What a host has to do

Handle `KnockedOut` and `knock_out_not_melee` (checklist items 1 and 2). Every
default leaves the 0.7 behaviour in place.

### New behaviour

- `PlayerIntent.knock_out=True` on an `attack` pulls every melee blow of the
  intent (SRD 5.2 Knocking Out a Creature). A blow that would drop a creature
  to 0 Hit Points leaves it at 1 instead: `DamageApplied(knock_out=True)`,
  `KnockedOut(target_id, attacker_id)`, `ConditionApplied("unconscious")`. It
  is not dying: no `Unconscious` marker and no death saves.
- The intent is refused with `knock_out_not_melee`, before anything is spent,
  unless every attack it resolves is a melee attack. Only the attack's own
  damage is pulled: a rider resolved by a saving throw after the hit can still
  kill. Other intent types ignore the flag, so a melee spell attack kills.
- A creature immune to Unconscious is left at 1 Hit Point and stays conscious;
  a summon still leaves at 0.
- A knocked-out creature stays out until it regains Hit Points (any healing,
  Regeneration included, wakes it Prone) or drops to 0 Hit Points. While out it
  is in `LiveCombatView.knocked_out_ids`, no monster targets it, a host may not
  name it as `target_id`, and the end reason counts it with the dead on its
  side.
- At `end_combat`, `CombatOutcome.knock_outs` lists the creatures still out, in
  the order they went down, as `KnockOutRecord`s. A foe still knocked out is
  worth its XP, as a dead one is.
- First aid as an action, a knock-out by a melee spell attack, and a
  monster-driven turn pulling its blow are not modelled (BACKLOG.md).

## Kill attribution

### Behaviour changes

- `DeathRecord.killer_id` names the creature that dealt the killing damage,
  whoever's turn it was. On 0.7 it named the creature whose turn it was, so an
  opportunity attack that killed the mover recorded nobody and a legendary
  action recorded whoever's turn it interrupted. A spell construct's kill names
  its caster; a summon's kill names the summon. A death save the dying
  creature fails on its own turn, and a creature that killed itself, name
  nobody; damage that adds the third failure names the creature that dealt
  it. `KnockedOut.attacker_id` and
  `KnockOutRecord.attacker_id` follow the same rule.
- `Combatant.last_damaged_by` follows the same source, so a reaction that
  answers damage (Hellish Rebuke) can target an opportunity attack's reactor.
- `LiveCombatView.deaths` lists the deaths so far, in order, with that
  attribution: the records `CombatOutcome.deaths` will hold.

## Smaller changes

- A character already Unconscious from an effect (a sleep) that drops to 0 Hit
  Points now emits the `Unconscious` dying marker, as any character at 0 Hit
  Points does, and stays Unconscious when that effect ends; it used to wake at
  0 Hit Points.
- `end_combat` and `LiveCombatView.final_outcome` return a copy of the kept
  outcome each time, so mutating one no longer changes what a later call
  returns.

## nat20-bridge

### New behaviour

- `POST /v1/combat` takes `allies`, a list of monster slugs seated on the
  party's side; they share the foes' numbering. Allies alone answer 422.
- `POST /v1/combat/{cid}/advance-monster` takes an optional body
  `{"target_id": "<entity id>"}`. No body, `{}` or `{"target_id": null}`
  keeps the engine's pick; an illegal target answers 409 `target_invalid`; any
  other key answers 422.
- An intent body accepts `knock_out`; a non-melee attack answers 409
  `knock_out_not_melee`. A knock-out narrates as a line.
- `GET /v1/combat/{cid}` adds `party_ids`, `encounter_ids`, `knocked_out_ids`
  and `deaths`.

## New API

- `EncounterMemberSpec.side` and `Side` (`dnd5e_engine.specs`).
- `advance_monster_turn(..., target_id=None)`.
- `PlayerIntent.knock_out` and the `knock_out_not_melee` rejection reason.
- `KnockOutRecord` (top level) and `CombatOutcome.knock_outs`.
- `LiveCombatView.knocked_out_ids` and `LiveCombatView.deaths`.
- `KnockedOut` and `DamageApplied.knock_out` (`dnd5e_engine.events`).
- For hosts that resolve activities themselves:
  `ActivityResolutionContext.knock_out` and `knock_out_exempt_ids`, the same
  two keywords on `build_activity_context`, the `knock_out` keyword of
  `apply_damage`, and `is_melee_attack` (`dnd5e_engine.activities.attack`).
- `dnd5e_engine.testing.rng_factory`: a test-support context manager that
  scripts or records a combat's dice, Initiative included. It is process-wide
  for the block and not for production code.

## Moved seeded pins

With no `side`, no `target_id` and no `knock_out`, seeded fights produce the
same event logs as on 0.7, except where a monster's closing walk used to end on
or pass through another creature's cell, and where a `move` used to be refused
with a detour available. Three engine pins moved, each to the detour:

- `tests/test_c16_orchestrator.py`: the `blocked_path` pin is now a 10 ft
  detour; a walled-in destination still answers `blocked_path`, and a detour
  that costs too much answers `insufficient_movement`.
- `tests/test_orchestrator_grid_combat.py`: the wall between two adjacent cells
  is now a 25 ft detour.
- `tests/e2e/test_c16_spatial_targeting.py`, the cut-corner scene: one 10 ft
  move around the corner instead of a refusal.

`DeathRecord.killer_id` changes where a kill came from a creature other than
the current actor.
